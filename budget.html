Marketing budget allocation

Which brand deserves more of the budget and which one is wasting it, worked out from who actually registered and who actually walked in. Type your budget and the split updates.

How to read this. A brand is worth what it puts in the room, not what it puts on a form. So the split follows guests who attended (scanned at the door, Manchester and Birmingham), adjusted for how reliably each brand's sign-ups turn up and how many are repeat bookings. There are no spend figures per brand in our records, so this is a recommended split of a budget you set, not a cost-per-guest report. London had no door scan, so its sign-ups count as volume but not as proof. Scorp staff and guest tickets are internal and left out. Small brands (under 50 sign-ups at the two events) are marked Test: the sample is too small to judge.

How the number is worked out

Guests who came is the count scanned at the door at Manchester (5 Nov) and Birmingham (12 Nov), one per person. Anyone let in without a scan is not counted, so real turnout is a little higher for everyone.
Turnout = came ÷ registered. Vs avg compares a brand's turnout with all external brands together.
Verdict (brands with 50 or more registered): Scale up at 1.2× the average or better, Hold from 0.9× to 1.2×, Reduce from 0.7× to 0.9×, Waste below 0.7×. 10 to 49 registered: Test. Fewer than 10: no verdict.
Budget share (guest method) = guests who came × the brand's turnout against average (shrunk toward the average for small samples, so one lucky night does not win a big budget) × (1 − its repeat-booking rate). Shares add up to 100%. The sign-up method is the same budget split by registrations, shown for comparison.
Not used: Approved status (the team's choice, not the brand's), London (no scan), Canterbury (not held yet: 26 Nov / 2 Dec). When Canterbury and later events are scanned, add their counts to the lists at the top of this page's script and the split updates.
